# Contextual Side Panels for Surface-Specific Workspaces
#learning

## Insight
When a product has one primary deep-work surface and several adjacent reference or planning surfaces, a persistent side panel should be reserved for the deep-work surface and released back to the main canvas elsewhere.

## Why It Matters
Keeping the same inspector rail on every page makes the application feel polished at first, but it quietly wastes the most valuable screen area on surfaces that need broader browsing, editing, or comparison space. Contextual side panels preserve focus where they help and remove chrome where they do not.

## Reusable Practice
- Keep the inspector persistent on the primary drafting or editing surface where continuity context materially helps the core task.
- Remove or collapse the inspector on adjacent surfaces when the main value comes from a wider content canvas.
- Preserve cross-surface consistency through shared navigation and styling, not by forcing the same three-column layout everywhere.
- Use surface-specific metrics and framing to make adjacent pages feel intentional without requiring entirely custom tools for v1.

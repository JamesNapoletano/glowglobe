# Hierarchical Scene Rails Read Better Than Split Active Panels
#learning

## Insight
When a writing workspace keeps both the left navigation rail and the right inspector visible, the scene rail becomes too constrained for separate chapter and active-chapter containers. A single hierarchical chapter-and-scene navigator is easier to scan and wastes less width on framing.

## Why It Matters
Authoring tools often operate in narrow center-column budgets on laptop screens. If the structural navigator spends that budget on stacked panel chrome and repeated headings, scene browsing starts to feel cramped even before the actual list density is too high.

## Reusable Practice
- Use one clear hierarchical navigator instead of splitting chapter selection and active-chapter scenes into separate framed blocks.
- Keep scene creation inline with the active chapter so action placement reinforces hierarchy.
- Prefer short multi-line clamping for structural labels in narrow rails over unrestricted wrap-anywhere behavior that can look broken.
- Spend limited rail width on list content first and supporting chrome second.

# Sharper Workspace Shells Through Vertical Density Control
#learning

## Insight
In dense authoring products, a more professional UI often comes less from adding polish details and more from removing softness: sharpen shared radii, trim nested padding, and let complex subsections stack vertically before they become cramped.

## Why It Matters
Editorial and CRUD-heavy interfaces quickly lose usable room when several card layers, pills, and side-by-side panels all compete inside the same shell. A cleaner vertical rhythm keeps the interface calmer and makes the primary content area feel more intentional.

## Reusable Practice
- Tighten radius at the theme level first so every shared surface benefits immediately.
- Reduce duplicate framing around primary canvases and form-heavy areas before tuning typography.
- In nested workspace layouts, prefer vertical stacking over breakpoint-driven multi-column density once a child surface already contains its own split layout.
- Treat primary panels, secondary panels, and inline support sections as different surface tiers so spacing choices stay consistent.

## Purpose
Define the canon reference layer that helps authors maintain terminology, history, and recurring concepts across manuscript and planning work.

## Important State and Dependencies
- Core entities: `Glossary Entry`, `Lore Note`, `History Note`
- Depends on project ownership and cross-linking with manuscript content
- May connect to characters, locations, technologies, and plot threads

## Process or Interaction Model
The author records key terms and canon notes, links them to relevant scenes or entities, and uses them as a searchable source of truth while drafting and revising.

## Decisions and Rationale
- A separate lore/glossary layer reduces duplication across other worldbuilding surfaces.
- Structured terms improve consistency in recurring names and concepts.
- This area should remain tightly linked to manuscript content so it serves the active writing workflow.

## Risks and Failure Modes
- Canon notes may sprawl without enough structure.
- Duplicate terminology can emerge if references are not normalized.
- If disconnected from drafting, the glossary becomes archival instead of useful.

## Verification or Evidence
- Terms can be created, referenced, and retrieved consistently.
- Lore notes can link to scenes, characters, places, or technologies.
- Authors can distinguish canon from speculative notes or open questions.

## Relationship to Other Areas
This area connects broadly across `Writing Studio`, `Characters`, `Technology`, `Environment and Planet`, and `Plot and Structure`.

## Open Questions
- How lore notes and `Sociums` should divide canon authority for organized groups
- Whether canonical status labels are needed in v1

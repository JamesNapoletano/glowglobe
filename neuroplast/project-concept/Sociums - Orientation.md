# Sociums - Orientation
#project-concept

## Purpose
Store structured information about factions, clans, guilds, kingdoms, corporations, religions, and other organized groups so continuity questions about power blocs stay easy to answer.

## Current Reality
Sociums are now a first-class product surface with a typed editor, richer fields, and dedicated CRUD workflows instead of living only as loose lore notes.

## Desired Outcome
The author can quickly capture what a group is, how it operates, who leads it, what it wants, and how it relates to allied or rival powers.

## Important Actors or Stakeholders
- Primary author
- Story-world groups and organized institutions as tracked narrative entities

## Key Artifacts or Interfaces
- Socium profile — identity, type, scope, leadership, and summary
- Strategy notes — goals, beliefs, resources, methods, and internal conflicts
- Diplomacy notes — allies, rivals, reputation, and additional continuity notes

## Constraints
- Must stay quick to browse inside the shared non-writing workspace pattern
- Must support multiple group flavors through a bounded type dropdown rather than many custom forms
- Must fit the local-first, sync-ready project aggregate model

## Open Questions
- When allied and rival groups should become relational links instead of multiline text
- Whether membership and hierarchy modeling should become a later dedicated extension

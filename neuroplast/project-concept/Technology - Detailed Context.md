## Purpose
Define how the app models systems, inventions, and rules so authors can maintain believable internal logic across stories.

## Important State and Dependencies
- Core entities: `Technology Entry`, `Rule`, `Dependency`, `Usage Example`
- Depends on project ownership and manuscript linking
- May connect to locations, factions, glossary terms, and timeline events

## Process or Interaction Model
The author records a technology or system, defines how it works, what it requires, where it appears, and what limitations govern it. Those entries are then referenced during drafting and planning.

## Decisions and Rationale
- Technology should be modeled as structured entries because consistency depends on explicit rules and limits.
- Linking technology to scenes and world data supports continuity and plot plausibility.
- The model should remain broad enough to cover both literal technology and adjacent rule systems.

## Risks and Failure Modes
- Entries may become vague if structure is too loose.
- A too-rigid schema could fail for nontraditional systems.
- Weak linkage to manuscript content could reduce practical usefulness.

## Verification or Evidence
- A technology entry can define purpose, rules, limitations, and linked appearances.
- Technology references resolve from manuscript and planning contexts.
- The author can distinguish canon rules from speculative notes.

## Relationship to Other Areas
Technology links to `Writing Studio`, `Environment and Planet`, `Lore and Glossary`, `Plot and Structure`, and potentially `Characters` when systems are character-dependent.

## Open Questions
- Whether factions or organizations should become a first-class separate entity later
- Whether visual dependency diagrams belong in v1 or later

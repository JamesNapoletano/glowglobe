## Purpose
Define the structured worldbuilding model for places and environments so settings remain vivid, navigable, and internally consistent.

## Important State and Dependencies
- Core entities: `Location`, `Region`, `Planet`, `Culture`, `Travel Note`
- Depends on project ownership, manuscript linking, and lore consistency
- May connect to timeline events, factions, characters, and technology entries

## Process or Interaction Model
The author defines worlds and places, captures salient environmental and social traits, records travel or geographic assumptions, and references those details while drafting and planning scenes.

## Decisions and Rationale
- Settings should be structured enough to support continuity and scene recall.
- Place hierarchy matters because novels often move between nested settings.
- Worldbuilding should remain adjacent to the manuscript rather than isolated in a disconnected encyclopedia.

## Risks and Failure Modes
- Overly deep world models may create maintenance burden.
- Unclear place hierarchy could confuse linking and navigation.
- Missing travel logic can create continuity breaks.

## Verification or Evidence
- A location can be linked to scenes and characters.
- Place hierarchy can represent local-to-global setting relationships.
- Environment details remain accessible during drafting and revision.

## Relationship to Other Areas
This area links strongly with `Writing Studio`, `Characters`, `Technology`, `Lore and Glossary`, and `Corkboard and Timeline` when events are place-dependent.

## Open Questions
- Whether maps are visual-only attachments or structured entities later
- Whether factions and cultures should be separated in v2
